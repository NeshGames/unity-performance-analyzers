# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be civil to the people you are talking to, and be exacting about the work. Those are not
in tension: the whole point of a project like this one is that a claim can be wrong without
the person who made it being at fault, and saying so plainly is a service.

Concretely, in issues, pull requests and reviews:

- **Argue about the claim, not the person.** "This measurement has no control group" is the
  review this project wants. "You clearly do not understand IL2CPP" is not the same
  sentence and does not carry the same information.
- **Say what would change your mind.** A rejection that names the missing evidence is
  actionable; one that names a feeling is not.
- **Assume the other person is reading carefully and has less context than you**, rather
  than the reverse.
- English or Traditional Chinese are both fine, in either direction. Do not treat someone's
  fluency as evidence about their competence.

Not acceptable, and not open to debate: harassment, personal or demographic attacks,
sexualized language or imagery, deliberate intimidation, publishing anyone's private
information, or persistently pushing past a clear "no".

## Scope

This applies in every space the project occupies — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, and any venue where you are recognisably acting on the project's behalf.

## Enforcement

Report a problem by opening a private security advisory on this repository, which reaches
the maintainers and nobody else. Reports are read; the reporter's identity is not shared
beyond what is needed to act.

Responses run from a private word, through editing or removing the content, to a temporary
or permanent ban. Which one is chosen depends on severity and on whether it is a pattern.
The maintainers decide, and will say what was decided to the person reporting it.
